Vision-Guided Camera Gimbal In progress

A two-axis gimbal that finds a soccer ball and keeps it centered without anyone driving it — my senior design project (ECE 445) at UIUC. A camera on the gimbal streams frames to a laptop, an object-detection model locates the ball, and the resulting movement commands go over Ethernet to a custom PCB that closes the loop on two brushless motors, with a laser kept on the ball as the visible proof it's tracking.

I work on the firmware and part of the vision. The firmware is an STM32 running the board's state machine: commanding the motor driver over PWM, handling the link back to the laptop, and persisting motor position and calibration to a non-volatile memory IC so the gimbal doesn't lose its bearings across power cycles. On the vision side, detecting the ball and turning its position in frame into the movement commands the board acts on.

It runs on a custom 24 V board that feeds an inverter stage for the motors and steps down to 5 V and then 3.3 V for the logic, with a PI filter sized against the inverter's 25 kHz switching to keep that noise off the rail the analog signals reference. We're building to three targets: hold the ball inside the center 80% of the frame for 30 seconds, update position at least 5 times a second, and re-center within 1 second of the ball re-entering frame.

Persistence-Aware MoE Serving In progress

Mixture-of-experts models route each token to a few experts, and that routing is lopsided: in OLMoE, a small handful of experts absorb a disproportionate share of the tokens, so one GPU queues up while others idle. The usual fix is replicating hot experts, but a replica you create for a hotspot that evaporates has cost you transfer time and memory for nothing.

So we measured whether the imbalance actually persists. Routing patterns within a single workload have about 0.96 cosine similarity, against roughly 0.67 across different workloads — the hotspots are recurring structure, not noise. The system we're building splits the decision in two: a slow path that creates a replica only when expected benefit over a persistence horizon beats the transfer and memory cost, and a fast path that spreads tokens across whatever replicas exist based on live queueing and profiled execution time. The target is the case where you have one strong GPU and one older one sitting idle — we're evaluating on a 4090 and a 3080 with a trace-replay executor. A course project with four teammates.

Operating System Kernel

I wrote a Unix-like kernel from scratch in C and RISC-V assembly. It does preemptive multitasking, Sv39 three-level paging with a separate address space per process, and a read-write filesystem (KTFS) with a write-back cache. On top of that sit fork, exec, and wait, and a shell of my own that handles I/O redirection, piping, and background jobs.

VirtIO block and UART drivers plus an ELF loader bring it up under QEMU: it boots from nothing, mounts a filesystem off an emulated disk, loads a binary into a fresh address space, and drops into user mode. The games it runs are unmodified 1970s BSD and Infocom programs compiled for RISC-V — the kernel underneath is the project. Recordings of it booting · Source
